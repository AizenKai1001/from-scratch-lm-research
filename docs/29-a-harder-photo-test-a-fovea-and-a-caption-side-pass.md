# 29. A harder photo test, a fovea at a fixed budget, and a caption side pass

## What this is

Snapshot dated 7 November 2026. This document continues document 28, where the small glance (the picture input of our eye pipeline, 448 by 192 pixels, or 84 picture tokens) carried generated shapes and easy photographs to the language side. Here we ask harder questions of it, and of its larger sibling Qwen3.5-2B, and test what format a running stream of pictures needs for captions.

Each test had a written rule with fixed bars, registered (a checksum and a time) before its script ran. Treat every result as an indication, not a proof, and misses are reported as plainly as passes. Photographs come from a public photo set with human-typed captions; shapes are generated by code. No model-written text was used as an answer key.

After the first draft, a separate read-only review by another AI agent went through the rules, scripts and result files. Its findings are built into this text, and the section "Corrections made before publishing" lists what changed.

> **CAVEAT, read first.** The photographs come from the COCO validation set, which is widely copied on the web. The model makers do not disclose the training data of these small models (the Qwen3.5-0.8B model card names no dataset), and a sibling model's technical report describes web image-caption pairs. So it is **plausible, and not checked either way**, that the tested models have met these photographs and their captions. Our controls show the models need the picture. They cannot separate seeing a picture from having met that photo and caption in training. A control on fresh photographs (section 5) found the same task about 15 points lower on photos uploaded after the models were released, still far above chance; it could not separate memory from caption style and weaker wrong options. Document 28 used the same photo set, so its photo numbers carry the same caveat.

## 1. Hard wrong captions and a sweep of picture size (test PERC-COCO-2)

**What we asked.** Document 28 found about 98 percent accuracy when the wrong captions were random photographs, an easy choice. Does the glance still work when the wrong captions share the main category? How many picture tokens does it need?

**What we did.** 500 photographs from the COCO 2017 validation set (**PUBLISHED**: secondary sources give CC BY 4.0 for the annotations and Flickr terms for the photographs; the official terms text was not retrieved). Each item has one photograph and four captions: its own human caption and three from photographs whose main object category (largest summed annotation area) is the same. Scoring as in document 28. Controls at 84 tokens: a grey picture, a picture of another category, and no picture. The same items were then run at 21, 40, 84, 189 and 336 tokens.

**What we found (FACT).** At 84 tokens: 0.850 for the 0.8B (95 percent interval 0.816 to 0.879) and 0.874 for the 2B (0.842 to 0.900). Controls (0.8B / 2B): grey picture 0.232 / 0.258; wrong picture 0.236 / 0.256; language only 0.236 / 0.240.

| Picture tokens | 0.8B accuracy | 0.8B minus 84-token (interval) | 2B accuracy | 2B minus 84-token (interval) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 21 | 0.764 | -0.086 (-0.118 to -0.056) | 0.806 | -0.068 (-0.094 to -0.044) |
| 40 | 0.806 | -0.044 (-0.072 to -0.018) | 0.860 | -0.014 (-0.030 to 0.000) |
| 84 | 0.850 | reference | 0.874 | reference |
| 189 | 0.862 | +0.012 (-0.010 to +0.034) | 0.878 | +0.004 (-0.012 to +0.020) |
| 336 | 0.862 | +0.012 (-0.010 to +0.032) | 0.886 | +0.012 (-0.004 to +0.028) |

The registered readings were BUDGET-SIZE none and GAIN none. The 2B at 40 tokens sits on the edge of the budget bar (its lower bound is just below -0.030, and the verdict flips at the fifth decimal), so we call it borderline and do not claim that 40 tokens cost nothing.

Two more facts from the same files. Accuracy rises with caption length (0.8B by thirds of caption length, shortest to longest: 0.826, 0.860, 0.876; 2B: 0.810, 0.915, 0.914). The score is a sum over the tokens of a caption, so short correct captions are at a disadvantage, and this is not part of any registered reading. And the time per look is flat below about 84 tokens (2B: 0.30, 0.29 and 0.32 seconds per item at 21, 40 and 84 tokens; 0.8B: 0.25, 0.26, 0.32), because text passes and fixed costs dominate. Fewer tokens did not make a look cheaper in our setup.

**What it means (GUESS).** In these tests the gains flatten above 84 tokens: from 84 to 336 tokens the 0.8B gains 1.2 points and the 2B 1.2 points, neither separated from zero. We did not fit a breakpoint, so "knee" would claim more than five points can show. Halving the tokens costs 1.4 to 4.4 points (**COMPUTED** from the table).

**What it does not show.** New pictures (see the caveat), other kinds of hard choice, faces, text, or moving scenes. The token counts are canvas counts, not picture counts: a 4:3 photograph fitted to the 448 by 192 canvas fills about 57 percent of its width (**COMPUTED**: 256 of 448 pixels), and the rest is grey padding, so roughly 48 of the 84 tokens carry photograph content (GUESS). We did not test a padding-free crop at the same budget.

## 2. A fovea at a fixed 40-token budget (test PERC-COCO-3)

**What we asked.** Eyes sample unevenly: sharp at the point of gaze, blurred at the edges. At the same 40 tokens, does a picture built that way beat a plain one, and does it matter where the detail goes?

**What we did.** The same 500 items at 40 tokens, about half the cost of the 84-token glance in tokens (not in time, see section 1). Four arms: plain; saliency fovea (the magnified area placed on the most eye-catching spot, from a classic saliency map); centre fovea; and random-spot fovea as a control. The warp stretches each axis separately, so the magnification runs along the chosen row and column across the whole picture: it is a cross of magnification, not a round spot. The magnified area is about 1.7 times plain and the edges about 2.3 to 2.5 times squeezed.

Before any model result, a check on 60 photographs showed the saliency peak on the picture border, a method artefact. We zeroed the map within 8 of its 64 pixels at each edge (12.5 percent; the addendum had said 12 percent), registered as an addendum before any arm ran. The first script version was never run on a model.

**What we found (FACT).** Accuracy (0.8B / 2B): plain 0.818 / 0.858; saliency fovea 0.790 / 0.840; centre fovea 0.810 / 0.856; random-spot fovea 0.750 / 0.814. Paired differences, with 95 percent intervals:

- Saliency minus plain: -0.028 (-0.058 to +0.002) for the 0.8B; -0.018 (-0.044 to +0.004) for the 2B. Not better.
- Saliency minus random spot: +0.040 (+0.008 to +0.074) for the 0.8B, separated from zero; +0.026 (-0.004 to +0.056) for the 2B, not separated.
- Centre minus plain: -0.008 and -0.002. Equal.
- Random spot minus plain: -0.068 (-0.102 to -0.036) and -0.044 (-0.070 to -0.016). A wrong spot clearly hurts.

Registered reading, 2B: FOVEA-NEUTRAL. FOVEA-HELPS needed the saliency arm at least 3 points above plain, with a lower bound above zero. FOVEA-HURTS needed it more than 2 points below plain, with an upper bound below zero. Neither held, and the 2B difference lies in the neutral range. For the 0.8B the point estimate (-2.8 points) is past the HURTS threshold but its upper bound (+0.002) is not below zero, and it is outside the neutral range, so **no registered label applies**. The script printed NEUTRAL because its last branch defaulted to it. We report the 0.8B as UNCLASSIFIED: slightly worse than plain, with an interval that touches zero.

**What it means (GUESS).** For a vision part trained on undistorted pictures, a warped 40-token look gave no gain at these settings. The warp's cost is paid back only in part. The saliency spots fell in the central third of the picture 24 percent of the time (uniform spots: 11 percent), so "saliency beats a random spot" may partly be "a more central spot beats a random spot". The centre arm equals plain, so centrality alone does not hurt, but a saliency-matched random control was not run.

**What it does not show.** Other warp strengths, other saliency models, learned saliency, gaze that moves over time, or the 84-token budget. One strength (0.6), one width (0.18), one budget. A trained fovea has not been tested.

## 3. Does the label "Caption:" change what the model writes? (test PERC-CAP-1)

**What we asked.** Can the model write what it sees after nothing more than the bare label "Caption:" placed after a picture? No instruction, only the label.

**What we did.** The same 500 hard photographs at 84 tokens. The model writes greedily (always the single most likely next word), up to 24 tokens. Each line is scored against four human captions by word overlap (content-word F1, 0 to 1; no model judged the lines). Comparisons: the same model on a grey picture; a random same-category human caption; and the photograph's own first human caption, left out of the references, as the human level. The readable share is the share of lines passing a check.

**What we found (FACT).** 0.8B: lines score 0.310; grey picture 0.011; random caption 0.163; human level 0.463. Minus the random caption: +0.147 (+0.129 to +0.164). Minus the grey picture: +0.299 (+0.286 to +0.311). Readable share 0.932. Registered reading: SAYS-WHAT-IT-SEES.

2B: lines score 0.230; minus the random caption +0.067 (+0.047 to +0.088); minus the grey picture +0.226; readable share 0.632. Registered reading: PARTLY. Its score is 0.498 of the human level (**COMPUTED**), a hair under the 0.50 bar; with about fifteen interval verdicts in the photo tests and no correction for that, treat borderline results as exploratory.

**What it means (GUESS).** With only the label, the picture changes the line (a grey picture gives almost nothing) and the line beats a random caption of the same category. That is the part the test supports.

**What it does not show.** A good description, and the registered word "sees" should not be read as a quality score. Four limits of the measure, found in review:

- The overlap score uses word sets, so precision falls as a line gets longer. The model's lines in the stream runs are longer than the human references (6 to 16 words), so part of the gap to the human level, and part of the gap to the grey picture, may be length or style and not content. Per-item outputs were not saved, so a cross-scoring check (a line against the references of another photograph) cannot be done without a re-run.
- The stop-word list removes counts and direction words ("two", "one", "up", "down", "near", "next"), so the score cannot tell whether a line got "two dogs" right.
- "Readable" is a character filter (it rejects quotes, colons, brackets and non-ASCII letters) and checks nothing about whether a sentence finished. The 2B's 0.632 may be partly this artefact, because its lines are longer. No hand count of lines has been written.
- Word overlap cannot judge whether a sentence is true. One label and one length cap were tried. A line the model has memorised from training would also score well (see the caveat).

## 4. The format finding: the label inside a running stream of pictures

**What we asked.** Does the label still work inside a long running stream of pictures, where a line is written after each picture and fed back into the stream?

**What we did.** Two runs of a few minutes of moving pictures from a private source, one picture every two seconds, with a line after each. The stream has a header and a diary-like layout, and also carries other input lines that these tests do not examine. We tried five layouts against a check registered before the runs (readable share, distinct share, line length and empty lines, with bars fixed in advance). That check tests format only; it does not test content. The layouts were designed on the first stream (so it is a tuning stream); the second was run only on the final layout. The numbers from these two runs are not published, because the material they come from is private; only what they showed is described.

**What we found (FACT).**

- Layout 1 (label on its own line after the stream's line break): every caption empty, in both runs.
- Layout 2 (label straight after the picture's end marker): every caption empty.
- Layout 3 (layout 2 plus a hand-set decoding constraint: no line-break token in the first four tokens): a line for every picture, all one identical line.
- Layout 4: a separate short pass that sees only the picture and the label, in a fresh cache. Its line then enters the stream as an input line. We call it the **caption side pass**.
- Layout 5: layout 4, each line stopped at its first full stop, with a cap of 24 new tokens. It passed the registered format check.

Review found that layout 5 did not do what its check suggested. Many lines hit the 24-token cap, and a large share contained no full stop at all, so they stopped in the middle of a phrase. The readable check counted these as readable because it only looks at characters. Raising the cap to 40 tokens (layout 6, same everything else) fixed it: almost every line then ended with sentence punctuation and none was cut at the cap, in both runs. The exact-distinct share hides near-duplicates: counting a line as a duplicate when its content words overlap an earlier line by 0.6 or more, a sizeable share of the lines were near-duplicates, and in the second run the near-distinct share fell below the 0.80 bar we had used for exact strings. Replay ran at about real time (one offline replay of a stored file per stream, not a live measurement, no repeats).

**Is each line about its own picture?** A shuffled-picture check compared a line's content-word overlap with the lines of neighbouring pictures (within 60 seconds) against its overlap with lines of far pictures. The difference was positive in both runs, but the registered bar (at least +0.10 in both, with the lower bound above zero) was not met in the second by a hair. **Picture-specificity is NOT SHOWN** by that bar, and neighbouring pictures resemble each other in any video, so this is weak evidence either way. Many lines start the same way. For the plain Caption test in section 3 the picture dependence is shown (grey picture 0.01 against 0.31); for the stream captions it is not.

**What it means (GUESS).** The label works when the model has seen only the picture and the label. Inside a long text, earlier lines pull the next one toward their own pattern. A separate short pass avoids that, at the cost of a second pass for each picture.

**What it does not show.**

- That the stream "remembers its own words", or that the captions are an "inner voice" or "thinking". The side pass does not read the stream. The stream only receives each line as an input line, and a probe that swaps a line and measures the change in what the stream predicts next was not run.
- That the lines are correct. They are generic scene descriptions from one 0.8B model, not checked against labels.
- That any of this holds on other material: all caption tests above used still photographs, and the stream runs used two short pieces of moving pictures.

## 5. The same hard choice on photographs the models cannot have seen (test PERC-FRESH-1)

**What we asked.** The review said COCO may be in the training data. Do the hard-choice numbers survive on photographs that cannot be?

**What we did.** 431 photographs uploaded to Wikimedia Commons at least 7 days after the Qwen3.5 repositories were created (cut-off 7 March 2026; the creation date can precede the public release), with captions typed by the uploaders (**PUBLISHED**: licences CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA; we keep the photos private and do not redistribute them). A script built the set with fixed filters: capture date after the cut-off, 6 to 16 word English captions, bot-like uploaders and template captions dropped, at most 8 photos per uploader. We made a second build after the first came out at 247 photos, below the 300 the rules asked for, because its template filter was broader than intended; the change was written down before any model ran on a fresh photo. Both sets (fresh, and 431 of the COCO items) use the same option rule: the three wrong captions are the other captions in the set with the most content words in common, so pool size and kind of difficulty match. Scoring as in section 1, at 84 picture tokens, with a grey picture and no picture as controls. The rules fixed the readings before the run.

**What we found (FACT).** Accuracy of picking the right caption (chance 0.25), with 95 percent Wilson intervals:

| | 0.8B COCO | 0.8B fresh | 2B COCO | 2B fresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| With the picture | 0.80 (0.76 to 0.84) | 0.65 (0.61 to 0.70) | 0.84 (0.81 to 0.88) | 0.69 (0.64 to 0.73) |
| Grey picture | 0.26 | 0.26 | 0.23 | 0.23 |
| No picture | 0.12 | 0.17 | 0.13 | 0.17 |

Fresh minus COCO: -0.146 (-0.204 to -0.088) for the 0.8B and -0.156 (-0.211 to -0.100) for the 2B. The registered reading was INCONCLUSIVE: the hold needed the lower bound at or above -0.10, and the drop needed the upper bound below -0.10; the 2B upper bound is 0.0002 above that line. Both models pass the "sees" test on the fresh photographs (at least 0.50 with the controls at most 0.35). A second AI-agent review added two points that make the drop look larger than the verdict word suggests. First, the 2B upper bound is only 0.0002 above the line, and in about a third of alternative bootstrap draws that cell would read DROP. Second, in an unregistered re-analysis that leaves out the 62 fresh items whose wrong options had to be filled in at random, fresh minus COCO is -0.18 (0.8B) and -0.19 (2B), with upper bounds of -0.12 and -0.13, below the line. So INCONCLUSIVE understates the drop: it is about 15 to 19 points.

**What it means (GUESS).** On photos that cannot be in the training data, both models still pick the right caption 65 to 69 percent of the time among similar wrong ones, and the controls stay at or below chance, so picture content does reach the language side on new photographs. The same pipeline gives about 15 points less than on COCO, so part of the COCO numbers may be memory. The data cannot show how much. The no-picture control is below chance (0.12 to 0.17), so it is not a clean text-only baseline. The two sets differ in other ways: the fresh captions are longer after cleaning (15 against 10 tokens), 392 of 431 contain a digit or a capitalised word after the first (names and places a picture cannot tell), and their wrong options overlap less with the right caption (mean 0.165 against 0.224, and 62 fresh items needed a random fill). An exploratory split by that name feature (not registered) gave similar accuracy in both parts, but only 39 captions had none. Read the COCO numbers in sections 1 to 3 as an upper bound on typical photographs, and 0.65 to 0.69 as the figure on new photographs under this option rule.

**What it does not show.** That the models never saw a Commons photo (a photo can be an old scene uploaded late or a copy under another name); that the captions are human-written (code cannot prove it; about 35 of the 432 collected captions look terse or non-English); anything about topics beyond the set's skew (UK, Taiwan, US and Australia, a few community campaigns); or a comparison with the section 1 numbers, which used a different option rule (same main category).

## What we could not conclude, and caveats

- **COCO may be in the training data.** See the caveat at the top, and section 5 for the fresh-photo control (about 15 points lower on new photographs; memory, caption style and weaker wrong options are not separated). It is plausible and not checked. Photograph numbers here, and in document 28, describe familiar-type photographs, and an earlier internal conclusion that picture-to-language training would not be needed is withdrawn until a fresh-picture control exists.
- **Small tests.** Two sizes of one model family, one prompt label, one run per model, 500 items per photograph test, a 24-token cap in section 3. No judge was used for captions.
- **Not independent items.** The wrong captions and wrong pictures come from other photographs in the same pool, and the intervals are bootstrapped over items as if independent; a bootstrap over photographs would be wider. About fifteen interval verdicts were made with no correction, so borderline ones (2B at 40 tokens, 2B saliency against random spot, 2B caption score against 0.50) are exploratory.
- **The easy-end test is at the ceiling.** The generated shapes in document 28 reached 1.00, which shows simple features reach the language side. It says nothing about natural content, faces, text or moving scenes.
- **One kind of hard choice.** The wrong captions share only the main category.
- **Precision and hardware.** The 0.8B ran with 32-bit numbers on one graphics card and the 2B with 16-bit numbers on another. We did not separate precision effects.
- **Registration.** Rules were registered before each script ran. The border-mask addendum (section 2) followed a data check and preceded any model result. The narration check was set on the same scene it was tuned on (section 4).
- **Not tested.** Moving pictures beyond the two stream runs, faces, text, a trained fovea or learned saliency, other prompt labels, streams longer than five minutes, live capture, and a dependency probe.
- **AI-assisted.** Done with an AI assistant, as the README describes. The review above was done by another AI agent. No human has reviewed these numbers.

## Corrections made before publishing

The review changed these statements from the first draft:

- "Very likely in the training data" became "plausible, not checked", and the 98 percent result lost an internal conclusion that picture-to-language training is not needed.
- The 0.8B fovea verdict NEUTRAL became UNCLASSIFIED (no registered label fits; the script defaulted). The 2B budget verdict became borderline. The 12 percent border mask is 12.5 percent. The saliency spots are concentrated, not spread.
- A control on fresh photographs was added (section 5) after the first version of this document. A second review then made the fresh-photo drop larger in the text (15 to 19 points, not 15).
- "Knee of the curve" became "gains flatten above 84 tokens". The "twelve looks per second" value case was dropped, because look time is flat below 84 tokens.
- The caption score is described as "the picture changes the line and beats a random same-category caption", with its length, stop-word and readability limits stated.
- "Writes a readable line after every picture" became "a caption side pass writes a line after every picture, fed back as an input line", with the truncation and the 40-token fix. "Inner voice", "remembers" and "picture-specific" are stated as untested or not shown.

## Label legend

- **FACT**: measured by our runs, with setting and sample size where we have them. Intervals are 95 percent bootstrap intervals over items.
- **COMPUTED**: arithmetic on measured numbers.
- **PUBLISHED**: from a named published source or dataset description.
- **GUESS**: our reasoning, or anything beyond the tests.
- Verdict words (for example HARD-SEES, FOVEA-NEUTRAL, PARTLY) are the written rules' own labels. A missed bar is reported as a miss.

Where a claim could take two labels, we use the more cautious one.
